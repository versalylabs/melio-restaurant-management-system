# Melio website positioning plan

Owner review copy, 25 November 2026. Built from three pieces of work:
- two research briefs: the Kenyan restaurant market in 2025–2026, and Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers* and *$100M Leads*;
- a read of the live site and menu;
- an independent review by a second advisor.

Nothing here is built yet. Section 9 lists what the owner must decide first. **Decision 1, whether the menu prices are real, comes before everything else.**

## 1. The problem in one line

The website sells a fine-dining restaurant: "Sommelier Cellar", "100% Organic", "meats aged in-house", "concierge desk". The menu is good, affordable Kenyan food: chapati KES 30, githeri 120, beef stew 180, pilau 150–210, nyama choma 280. The middle item costs about KES 150. Customers who want fine dining leave disappointed, and customers who want a good, fair-priced plate never find out Melio is for them.

**Blocker:** the menu looks like demo data, so its prices may not be real.
- Some prices are odd: Mango Juice 79.99, Mukimo 129.98.
- Pizza costs 350–380 while burgers cost 850–950.
- Coke (150) is close to Tusker Malt (180).
- Beef Pilau is listed under Breakfast.

The price-led positioning below only works once the owner confirms real prices and what a full plate costs (for example stew plus ugali, about KES 225).

## 2. What the market says

- **Money is tight.** Inflation reached 7.3% in October 2026, with food up 10.2% (Citizen).
- **Office workers are trading down.** They are moving from KES 300–800 restaurant lunches to smokies and samosas (Citizen, July 2026). A CBD lunch with a drink averages about KES 636 (Expatistan).
- **Melio is well below that.** A full plate costs roughly KES 225–300 even with ugali or rice, less than half the norm. This is Melio's strongest fact, and the site never says it.
- **Price alone won't win the CBD.** Kibandas sell plates at KES 100–200, so Melio wins on proper food, a clean seat, speed, M-Pesa and a receipt. Price is the proof, not the identity.
- **Eating out is still growing.** Accommodation and food services grew 15.6% in 2025 and 14.7% in Q1 2026 (KNBS), and the chains keep opening branches.
- **The three branches serve different people.**
  - Moi Avenue (CBD): price-sensitive office workers, traders, students and commuters.
  - Westlands: offices, young professionals, expats and nightlife; the priciest dining area.
  - Kilimani: middle-income families and residents.
- **Search and reviews drive choice.** People choose on Google Maps reviews and on dishes they saw on TikTok and Instagram.
- **Top food-app searches** in Kenya in 2025 were fried chicken and pizza.
- **Delivery apps are expensive.** Glovo, Uber Eats and Bolt Food take a large commission (reported at up to 30%, not confirmed), so direct ordering protects the margin.
- **Customers expect to pay by M-Pesa.** Payments to a Buy Goods till are free for the customer; check Melio's merchant fees on small tickets.
- **Offices compare per-head prices.** They drop caterers over repetitive, carb-heavy menus.
- **Claims carry legal risk.** False or misleading claims about quality or composition are covered by Consumer Protection Act s.12 and Competition Act s.55 (fines up to KES 10 million). Unproven "organic" or "aged in-house" claims are a legal risk as well as a credibility one.

Market figures are for planning only. None of them go on the site.

## 3. Positioning

- **Who Melio is for:** people in Nairobi who want proper Kenyan food, cooked fresh, at a fair price, at lunch, at home or for the team.
- **The promise:** proper food, fair price, fast and easy. Never "cheap".

**Hero (recommended):**
- Headline: **"Proper Kenyan lunch. Ready when you walk in."**
- Subline: "Stew, pilau and nyama choma from KES [verified full-plate price]. Order ahead, pay by M-Pesa, skip the queue. Moi Avenue · Westlands · Kilimani."
- The "from" price must be a plate someone can actually buy at that price. An unreachable "from" price is the misleading-claim risk above.

How Hormozi's Value Equation shows up on the page:

| Lever | On the Melio site |
|---|---|
| Dream outcome (what they get) | A filling, tasty lunch without the queue; lunch sorted for the team. Sell the occasion, not the plate. |
| Likelihood (why believe it) | Real Google reviews, real photos of Melio's own plates, prices shown, and the hygiene licence shown only if current for each branch. |
| Time delay (how fast) | Honest ready-by times for pickup and delivery per branch; instant booking confirmation. |
| Effort (how easy) | One tap to order, M-Pesa prompt, "order again", branch remembered, a WhatsApp button. |

## 4. Who to target, in order

1. **Weekday breakfast and lunch at Moi Avenue (CBD).** The best fit: frequent, nearby, and the menu fits.
2. **Office lunch for Westlands and CBD offices.** One buyer feeds many people, every week.
3. **Home delivery (Kilimani).**
4. **After-work grills (Westlands):** wings, burgers, grilled chicken and steak.
5. **Later:** celebrations and family platters, once those products exist.

Message per branch:

| Branch | Message | Price anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Moi Avenue | "Breakfast and lunch in the CBD, ready when you arrive." | Lowest real plate price |
| Westlands | "Lunch for the office. Grills after work." | About KES 250–300 (combo). Not the lowest plate: a very low anchor here signals low quality and undercuts the grill items. |
| Kilimani | "Proper home food, delivered." | Delivery combo price |

One Melio brand, with a different message per branch.

## 5. Offers

These follow Hormozi's Grand Slam Offer method: list the customer's problems, turn each into a solution, bundle the high-value, low-cost ones and give the bundle a name. **Every price, item and perk is a placeholder for the owner.** The menu today is à la carte, so building combos is the real product work.

**Build first:**

| Offer (working name) | Problem → what's in it | Button |
|---|---|---|
| **CBD Lunch Combo** | Queues and unknown cost → one price for a plate with starch and a drink, ordered ahead, paid by M-Pesa, ready at a chosen time | Order pickup |
| **Office Lunch** | Repetitive menus, late food, invoicing → per-head packages, a rotating weekly menu, a delivery slot, a KRA eTIMS invoice, quote within 2 hours | Get a quote (existing "Corporate Catering" enquiry) |
| **Commuter Breakfast** | No time in the morning → chai plus mandazi or chapati plus eggs at one price (items already on the menu) | Order pickup |

**Next, once the first three work:**
- **Lunch Pass:** pay for 5 lunches by M-Pesa and get 6. Cash up front, and it builds a habit. This is Hormozi's continuity offer.
- **Melio at Home:** clear delivery zone and time, with the existing order-tracking page. Only say "no app markup" if app prices really are higher.

**Later:** Family Feast (needs a platter on the menu) and Birthday Table (needs someone to run events).

## 6. Guarantee, honest urgency and bonuses

- **Guarantees Melio can keep:**
  - "Wrong or missing item? We replace it or refund it."
  - "Pre-ordered lunch not ready at your time? Your chai is on us." This covers pickup only, because the kitchen controls it and Nairobi traffic doesn't. Use chai, not a soda: a KES 150 drink on a KES 180 plate is too much.
  - No catering time guarantee until delivery is proven. No "love it or it's free".
- **Honest urgency:**
  - "Today's special", marked "Sold out" by staff when it's gone.
  - A lunch order-by time ("order by 11:30 for 12:30").
  - No fake countdowns or invented "only 2 left".
- **Bonuses, not discounts:** free chai or mandazi on a first online order. Repeated discounts teach people to wait and eat thin margins.

## 7. Getting leads (*$100M Leads*)

In order, for a small restaurant:
1. **Google review link** on the order confirmation and the receipt.
2. **Phone number with a consent tick at checkout** ("Send me Melio's specials by SMS/WhatsApp"). M-Pesa orders already collect the number.
3. **WhatsApp button** on the site. The feature exists; it needs the number set.
4. **SMS/WhatsApp to opted-in customers only** (weekly special, lunch pass), using the existing automated messaging. Marketing to people who didn't opt in breaches the Data Protection Act (s.37), and the regulator has fined businesses for it.
5. **Offline, month one:** flyers and visits to offices within about 500 m of Moi Avenue, offering the lunch combo and the office lunch. This is Hormozi's cold outreach, and it will beat anything digital at first.
6. **Content:** short TikTok and Instagram clips of real plates with prices on screen, linked from the site.

**Later:** referral codes, a birthday club, a catering menu download, paid ads. Hormozi's "more, better, new": push the lunch offer until it stops growing, improve it, and only then add a new audience.

## 8. Website changes: build first

1. **Remove unproven claims and fix the review label.**
   - Badges to remove: "100% Organic / Local Harvest", "Sommelier Cellar", "Chef Crafted / Made to Order".
   - Copy to remove: "meats aged in-house", "concierge desk", "Heritage & Culinary Craft", "Cherished By Discerning Diners".
   - Keep only claims the owner confirms.
   - Reviews typed in by staff are labelled "Verified Guest". Change that to "Guest review".
2. **New hero.** The headline and subline from section 3, a branch picker, and Order, Book and WhatsApp buttons. The WhatsApp button is already built and only needs the number.
3. **Menu fixes.**
   - Lunch plates and combos first.
   - A loading placeholder instead of "No dishes found" and "0 dishes". Slow phones see that empty version today.
   - Real photos of the Kenyan dishes (owner to shoot).
4. **One lunch combo card and the guarantee line** near the Order button.
5. **Per-branch Google Maps and review links, consent tick at checkout, and a sticky Order/Book bar on mobile.**

Then:
- Per-branch message lines.
- The breakfast combo and lunch pass.
- Search page titles and descriptions per branch.
- Restaurant structured data, without rating markup while reviews are staff-entered.

**Not building (over-built for now):**
- An offers admin with on/off toggles; hard-code one or two cards instead.
- A portions-left counter; a manual "Sold out" is enough.
- A referral system.
- A separate catering section; the enquiry form already covers it.

## 9. Decisions the owner must make first

1. **Menu prices:** are they real, and what does a full plate cost (with ugali or rice, and with a drink)? Do Westlands prices differ?
2. **Claims:** which are true (organic, aged meat, wine list, chef-crafted)?
3. **Combos:** the CBD lunch combo and breakfast combo, with contents, price and margin; office lunch per-head prices.
4. **Logistics:** delivery zones and times per branch, the lunch order-by time, and who approves refunds at each branch.
5. **Branch details:** the WhatsApp number, each branch's Google Maps link, and whether each branch's hygiene licence is current.
6. **Payments and messaging:** consent wording for SMS/WhatsApp, and whether M-Pesa is a till (Buy Goods) or a paybill.
7. **Alcohol:** whether Tusker and other alcohol should be sold online or for delivery. Check county and NACADA rules first.
8. **Photos:** a photo shoot of the real dishes.

## 10. Not verified

- **Hormozi principles:** based on reputable summaries, not the books themselves.
- **Market figures:** delivery commission rates, the share of complaints about late delivery, the Uber–Glovo deal, neighbourhood spending and catering prices are the weakest points. They are for planning only and must never appear as site copy.
- **Legal points:** from the statutes as summarised, not legal advice.
